## 🔐 Cybersecurity Journey

Okay, real talk: my cybersecurity origin story starts in ancient Sparta.

I grew up in Sparta, and Spartans were doing cryptography a *very* long time before it was cool. There's this thing called a scytale, basically a wooden stick ancient Spartan generals used to scramble secret military messages so they looked like nonsense to anyone without the matching stick. As a kid, learning that people were hiding secrets in plain sight thousands of years ago genuinely blew my mind. I didn't know it yet, but that was the moment.

That curiosity never really left. It just went quiet for a while, buried under a computer science degree, a master's thesis where I built encryption into actual hardware, time spent handling secure military communications, and years of building software and teaching people how to code. Cybersecurity was always the thing humming in the background. Now I'm finally chasing it properly. One step at a time. It feels less like starting something new and more like coming home.

## 🔬 MSc Thesis: Cryptography in Hardware

**Hardware implementation of a cryptographic system on an FPGA** · MSc in Advanced Computer Systems (Embedded Systems), University of Piraeus

- **What:** a C# desktop chat application whose messages were encrypted and decrypted in hardware, on an FPGA
- **Hardware:** Xilinx Spartan-3 FPGA, with **DES** and **Blowfish** implemented as dedicated hardware datapaths
- **Control:** a **PicoBlaze** soft processor coordinating the crypto engines and the communication with the PC
- **Feature:** real-time switching between DES and Blowfish, chosen by the user, without reprogramming the device
- **Why it still matters:** it gave me a low-level understanding of how symmetric ciphers work, how keys and data move through a system, and where the weak points are. It's the same thinking behind hardware security modules (HSMs), secure elements and the protections I later built into my own apps.
